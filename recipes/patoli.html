The story

A parcel of the season.

Patoli appears only a few times each year, around Nag Panchami and Ganesh Chaturthi. Turmeric leaves are sourced in advance, their scent already telling the house what is about to be made.

The filling is simple but never rushed: fresh coconut cooked down with jaggery until glossy and fragrant. Then comes the folding — a task that gathers people around the table as naturally as the final plate does.

Patoli is remembered as much for who helped press the dough onto each leaf as for the first warm bite. The work is part of the celebration.

“Some festival dishes are made to slow the whole house down.”

Ingredients

For 10–12 Patoli

  • 2 cups rice flour
  • ½ tsp salt
  • Hot water, as needed
  • 2 cups fresh grated coconut
  • 1 cup grated jaggery
  • ½ tsp cardamom powder
  • 10–12 turmeric leaves

Method

Fold gently, steam slowly.

  1. Cook the coconut and jaggery over a low heat until the mixture comes together. Stir in cardamom and cool.
  2. Mix rice flour with salt and enough hot water to make a soft, pliable dough. Cover it so it stays warm.
  3. Wash and dry the turmeric leaves. Lightly grease each leaf, then press a thin layer of dough onto one half.
  4. Add a spoonful of filling, fold the leaf over and press the edges closed.
  5. Steam for 12–15 minutes, until the dough is set and the leaf is fragrant. Cool briefly before unfolding.
A light brushing of Kokanee Hooman cold-pressed coconut oil → keeps the turmeric leaf from sticking before it goes into the steamer.
