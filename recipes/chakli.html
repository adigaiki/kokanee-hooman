Chakli,

The story

A spiral of memory.

Chakli arrives in many homes with the preparations for Diwali: tins are opened, flour is sifted, and someone always tests the first batch before the rest is allowed to cool.

In Goan and Konkani homes, the recipe shifts gently from family to family. One household leans into sesame, another prefers a softer centre, and every cook knows the right pressure for the spiral by feel rather than measurement.

It is the kind of snack made in quantity because it is never meant for one person alone. A plate travels to neighbours; a few pieces are packed for the road; the last crumbs are always claimed.

“The first chakli is a test. The next one is for the people you love.”

Ingredients

For one generous tin

  • 2 cups rice flour
  • ½ cup roasted gram flour
  • 2 tbsp white sesame seeds
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp chilli powder
  • Salt, to taste
  • Warm water, as needed
  • Oil for frying

Method

Take your time with it.

  1. Mix the rice flour, gram flour, sesame, cumin, turmeric, chilli powder and salt until evenly combined.
  2. Add warm water a little at a time, kneading until the dough is smooth, pliable and firm enough to press.
  3. Fit a chakli press with a star nozzle. Press the dough into small spirals on parchment or a clean cloth.
  4. Warm the frying oil over a medium heat. Slide in a few spirals at a time and fry until evenly golden and crisp.
  5. Drain well, cool completely, then store in an airtight tin so the crunch stays with you.
